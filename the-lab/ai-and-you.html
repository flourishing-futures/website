AI & You

A personal AI usage dashboard that turns an officer's activity into a reflective portrait of how they work with AI — surfacing habits, strengths and questions that encourage more intentional use, not merely greater adoption.


In-Planning

Context

Public-service AI adoption is currently understood through operational measures such as token consumption, cost and conversation volume, also often visible only to administrators. These measures show how much AI is being used, but reveal little about how well, thoughtfully or critically officers are using it.

Most officers have no private, intelligible view of their own AI-use patterns — no way to see whether they are developing as deliberate, skilled users of AI or simply becoming more dependent on it.

Hypothesis

If officers are given a private, intelligible view of their own AI-use patterns (including signals of steering, scrutiny and independent judgement) they may become more deliberate users of AI, rather than simply more frequent ones.

The Idea

A personal AI Usage Rewind that periodically turns an officer's activity into a reflective portrait of how they work with AI: surfacing habits, strengths and questions that encourage more intentional use, not merely greater adoption.

Useful signals could include: conversations where the officer challenged, corrected or redirected the AI; responses revised through multiple rounds rather than accepted immediately; instances where alternatives, sources of uncertainty were requested; patterns of usage; and a reflective prompt such as: "Where did AI expand your thinking this month — and where might it have narrowed it?"

The dashboard could present a weekly AI recap showing number of conversations, time spent, budget spent, top tasks and top subjects — alongside tips about AI limitations and prompts for self-reflection on where AI has genuinely helped.

Status

This experiment is currently in the planning stage. We are exploring the data architecture needed to surface meaningful AI-use patterns while preserving officer privacy, and designing the reflective prompts and dashboard interface.
