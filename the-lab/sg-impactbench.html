SG ImpactBench

Building on MIT Media Labs' ImpactBench, the goal of this project is to develop a framework that measures, assesses and presents human-impact metrics around AI usage — nuanced to individual models, with parameters informed by Singapore-specific context and public-sector work.


In-Flight

Overview

SG ImpactBench adapts MIT Media Lab's Open Benchmarks for Human Impact into a Singapore-contextualised framework for evaluating how AI systems affect the people who use them. While traditional AI benchmarks focus on model performance — accuracy, speed, safety — SG ImpactBench measures the human side: what happens to agency, judgement, capability, trust, and professional growth when people interact with AI over time.

The benchmarking framework covers multiple dimensions of human impact — from fairness and bias to societal and civic effects — and can be nuanced to individual AI models. This gives product owners, policymakers, and researchers a shared language and evidence base for understanding AI's effects on people.

Key Questions

  • How can we measure the human impact of AI systems deployed in Singapore's public sector in a rigorous, repeatable way?
  • What dimensions of human impact are most relevant to the Singapore context — and how do they differ from global frameworks?
  • Can a benchmarking framework help product owners and builders make more informed decisions about AI's effects on people?
  • How do different AI models compare in their effects on human agency, judgement, and capability?

Approach

The project adapts MIT's Open Benchmarks framework to Singapore's public-sector context. This involves localising the measurement dimensions, developing evaluation criteria that reflect Singapore's social norms and policy priorities, and validating the framework against real government AI deployments.

The benchmarking framework can be made accessible via a frontend that helps product owners and builders make informed decisions, policy-makers to define metrics of success, practitioners to develop playbooks, and researchers to track impact over time.
