Experiment · Design & Experimentation

Mindful Minutes

An AI reflection tool that invites public officers to intentionally pause, review and reflect on their human interactions — ensuring efficiency doesn't come at the cost of empathy, nuance and judgement.


In-Planning

Context

AI meeting tools (e.g. Confer) today are focused on solving the "what happened?" problem with the efficient capture, transcription and summarising of decisions and action-items. But meetings are also where our most meaningful interpersonal, creative, learning and growth moments happen. Purely outcome-focused AI risks flattening that.

When AI summarises a meeting into decisions and action items, it optimises for throughput. But the moments that matter most in professional development — a perspective that challenged your thinking, a subtle disagreement that was never resolved, a colleague's contribution you overlooked — are precisely the things that get stripped away.

Hypothesis

If AI playbacks weren't just "What was decided?" but also "How did I contribute, what did I miss, and what can I learn?", it can turn routine meeting recaps into opportunities for reflection and professional growth.

The Idea

With the same recording transcripts currently used for meeting summaries, this is an AI reflection tool that instead invites public officers to intentionally pause, review and reflect on their human interactions — ensuring efficiency doesn't come at the cost of empathy, nuance and judgement.

Potentially built as a layer on top of existing tools like Confer, or a native AI skill/prompt — it could prompt human reflection through questions like:

  • Was there a viewpoint that was overlooked, interrupted or insufficiently explored?
  • Did the discussion stay connected to the people or public outcome it is meant to serve?
  • What important issue remains unresolved beneath the agreed action items?
  • Did you change your mind at any point? What caused the shift?
  • Where can I improve? What can I learn from others? How could I connect with people better?

The tool would offer a weekly "Reflection on your meetings" view, surfacing patterns across multiple meetings and encouraging officers to reflect on their own contribution, growth areas, and interpersonal dynamics.

Status

This experiment is currently in the planning stage. We are exploring how to integrate reflective prompts into existing meeting tools, and designing the reflection experience to be genuinely useful rather than another notification to dismiss.
