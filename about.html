About the Programme

Flourishing Futures: AI for Human Possibility

A collaborative initiative across research institutions, government agencies and practitioners — working to ensure AI in public service expands human agency, judgement and possibility.

What's this, why now?

AI is rapidly reshaping how we live, create and work. While most conversation today is focused on adoption, efficiency-gains and cost, this programme exists to design for the 'hidden' factors.

Within public service, AI will change what public officers notice, what citizens are asked to trust, which forms of capability and care in Singapore's public products and services are automated, and which remain human.

The research and design this programme undertakes aims to shape those choices before they harden into systems. We examine where AI can genuinely enlarge human possibility — and where speed, scale or convenience may come at the cost of judgement, care, agency or public trust.

The aim is to push beyond AI Safety or Responsible AI, to surface the additional human insights and intentionally design human-AI experiences that work to make public officers more capable (not less).

Why now

Three reasons this work is urgent

1

The defaults are being set now

The government is aggressively driving adoption of AI tools at a scale few countries currently match. As AI becomes embedded in everyday public systems, early choices about automation, judgement and human involvement will become institutional norms, and later become much harder to reverse.

2

Public service cannot borrow its definition of 'good enough' from the market

Commercial incentives reward scale, speed and engagement; government must ask what strengthens agency, trust, care, capability and the public good. This must also work inside the unique realities of Singapore's policy owners, frontline officers and the general public.

3

Working teams need space to challenge, reshape and reimagine what success looks like

Before teams optimise how AI is deployed, we must ask whether the right relationship between human and machine has been imagined, and demonstrate what better interactions could look like.

Our Vision

We work to drive...

A future in which AI makes public service more capable and more humane: expanding (not diminishing) the agency, judgement and possibilities of the people who deliver it and depend on it.

The Landscape

A shift towards a more proactive approach

We work across a spectrum — from governing AI to imagining what AI could be for people.

Governing AI

Responsible AI

How do we make sure the use of AI is safe, secure, and fair?

The foundational layer — addressing known harms through guardrails, bias testing, safety standards and privacy protections. Led by teams like GovTech's Responsible AI practice and NAIG. This work is necessary and must be sustained.

Human-Agent Collaboration

How do we rethink workflows and workplaces so that humans and AI work well together?

What happens to a person in the moment of interacting with AI — the immediate cognitive, psychological, and behavioural effects. When an AI produces a confident output, does it help the person think, or does it create a bottleneck where humans defer rather than engage?

AI for Human Possibility

How do we build AI that expands human capability and agency?

Beyond safe collaboration, we ask whether AI can actively strengthen the people who use it — deepening professional judgement, surfacing learning opportunities, and preserving the formative struggle that builds expertise. This is the core focus of the Flourishing Futures programme.

Imagining AI

AI for Flourishing Futures

How do we ensure AI strengthens societal wellbeing and resilience in Singapore?

The broadest horizon — cumulative effects on social connection, cultural norms, democratic trust, and collective resilience. What kind of people and society will emerge from a decade of pervasive AI use? This is where measurement, design, and governance converge into long-term vision.

Outcomes

We work to develop...

A shared vocabulary around human-AI choices

Teams can clearly distinguish when AI should automate, augment, advise, or step aside — and discuss trade-offs without collapsing human values into "efficiency" or "risk."

New standards to measure human outcomes

Projects that deploy or depend on AI are assessed not only for accuracy, safety and cost, but for their effects on agency, judgement, capability, care, trust and access over time.

Public officers who are more capable, not just more productive

AI strengthens professional judgement, learning and discretion instead of quietly deskilling roles or narrowing them to machine supervision.

Human-centric principles embedded in AI delivery

Human-impact reviews, longitudinal research, citizen participation and frontline testing become part of how AI-enabled services are designed — not late-stage assurance exercises.


Citizens with greater agency in AI-mediated services

People can understand, question, contest and move beyond automated decisions, rather than being asked to trust systems they cannot meaningfully challenge.

A more engaged public conversation

Citizens are involved upstream in shaping what acceptable and desirable AI looks like, not consulted only after systems have already been built.

Stronger institutional confidence

Teams are able to make deliberate, defensible choices about AI, including the confidence to slow down, redesign or decline uses that do not serve the public well.

A portfolio of credible alternatives

The programme produces pilots, patterns and models that demonstrate what more humane, capable and publicly valuable uses of AI can actually look like.

Our Approach

Three modes of engagement

We approach this work through three complementary modes — each reinforcing the others.

Mode 1

Measurement & Insight

Through research partnerships, longitudinal studies and insight into real public-service contexts, build evidence that identifies not only how AI affects human agency, capability, relationships and trust, but also defines what better human-AI relationships could be.

Mode 2

Design & Experimentation

AI can do more for public service than make existing work more efficient: it can also strengthen human agency, judgement, learning, care, connection and trust. Here, we test our theories with small, working design interventions and tools that let people experience a different kind of AI, first-hand.

Mode 3

Governance & Ops

Translate evidence into shared standards, decision frameworks, practices, design patterns and capability. This includes clarifying accountability, governance, and policy advisory so human outcomes are built into how AI is commissioned, approved, deployed and reviewed across government.

Focus Areas

We focus on...

1

Public Officers

This is where public-service AI tools and systems are actively being developed and further being used to develop. We work to research, design and intervene here, to influence and equip our first line of defense: the teams leading and building public-service products.

2

Vulnerable Groups

Our research, design and collaboration focused on students, elderly, under-privileged and those in need of care — the groups where AI has the most potential benefits and risks. This is done in partnership with the relevant ministries and those delivering service on-the-ground.

3

Broader Society

The standards and evidence we build in government should eventually extend outward. The ultimate vision is to shape all AI experiences in Singapore through a combination of regulation and community influence.
