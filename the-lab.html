Design & Experimentation

Each experiment begins with a clear human-outcome hypothesis and tests it in the realities of public-service work. Across the portfolio, we hope to establish possibility, evidence, and translation.

Goal

Possibility

Provide forward-looking, alternative shapes of AI usage for officers, leaders and publics to experience, reference, critique and build-on.

Goal

Evidence

Through rigorous and intentional 'launching to learn', determine what factors and patterns genuinely help protect human capability and strengthen public value.

Goal

Translation

Identify the real design, data, trust, governance and conditions required to turn human-AI insights into usable design patterns, product principles and requirements that can shape AI development across government.

Current Experiments

Measurement · In-Flight

SG ImpactBench

Building on MIT Media Labs' ImpactBench, the goal of this project is to develop a framework that measures, assesses and presents human-impact metrics around AI usage — nuanced to individual models, with parameters informed by Singapore-specific context and public-sector work. This benchmarking framework can be made accessible via a frontend that helps product owners and builders make informed decisions, policy-makers to define metrics of success, practices to develop playbooks, researchers to track impact over time.

Design · In-Planning

AI & You

Public-service AI adoption is currently understood through operational measures such as token consumption, cost and conversation volume — visible only to administrators. These measures show how much AI is being used, but reveal little about how well, thoughtfully or critically officers are using it. If officers are given a private, intelligible view of their own AI-use patterns, they may become more deliberate users of AI, rather than simply more frequent ones. A personal AI Usage Rewind that periodically turns an officer's activity into a reflective portrait of how they work with AI: surfacing habits, strengths and questions that encourage more intentional use, not merely greater adoption.

Design · In-Planning

Mindful Minutes

AI meeting tools today are focused on solving the "what happened?" problem — efficient capture, transcription and summarising of decisions and action-items. But meetings are also where our most meaningful interpersonal, creative, learning and growth moments happen. If AI playbacks weren't just "What was decided?" but also "How did I contribute, what did I miss, and what can I learn?", it can turn routine meeting recaps into opportunities for reflection and professional growth. An AI reflection tool that invites public officers to intentionally pause, review and reflect on their human interactions — ensuring efficiency doesn't come at the cost of empathy, nuance and judgement.

Our Method

How we work

A key aspect of the programme is live experimentation — working with GovTech product development teams to apply human impact practices and rapidly test, learn, and iterate. Only by building and evaluating with real user behaviour can robust practices, capabilities, and design patterns emerge.

Each experiment assesses both operating outcomes (speed, throughput, quality) and human outcomes (agency, capability, judgement, dependency). We do not assume efficiency and human development are in conflict — we are testing whether the right design can achieve both.

Findings feed directly into the programme's three modes: updating the measurement frameworks, contributing evidence to the design pattern library, and informing governance and procurement standards.
